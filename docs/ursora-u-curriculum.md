# URSORA U: curriculum and evidence model

The course contains 50 lessons in 12 stages. The eight advanced additions cover account mechanics, event risk, portfolio stress, execution friction, option chains, payoffs, lifecycle obligations, and complete strategy samples. Original lesson IDs and saved answer identifiers remain stable.

Each original lesson has an authored worked example, common mistake, and independent explanation prompt. Guided references are distinct from independent skill checks. Conceptual exercises show a worked reference instead of an unrelated price chart. Focused practice can advance directly to remaining explanation sets.

## Evidence of learning

- Explanation and summary checks support instruction. A summary answer alone no longer completes dedicated practice.
- Practice completion records the learner's existing focused exercises. Earlier saved answers and completion records remain available.
- Nine independent demonstrations cover market mechanics, structure, indicators, thesis/event risk, sizing, execution, options, behavior, and strategy validation. All numeric and decision checks in a demonstration are required; attempts retain history. Numeric answers require entry, rather than recognition among distractors.
- Each skill has prerequisites. All lessons remain readable; advanced assessments require demonstrated prerequisites. Failed attempts provide explanations before a new case.
- First retrieval review is scheduled after three days; a successful delayed review schedules fourteen days. Early practice does not postpone a due review. These are learning defaults, not experimentally validated trading-readiness thresholds.
- Final assessment eligibility requires all nine skills and new-rubric price, indicator, and execution capstones. Thus a justified no-trade final session can rely on a separately observed execution demonstration.

## Capstone limits and safeguards

New attempts persist a seed and vary regime, scale, noise, activity and an authored event. These remain synthetic educational scenarios. Legacy unseeded attempts replay their original authored series and cannot silently award new-rubric completion.

Plans require positive finite trigger, invalidation and exit values in the correct order. Entries are reviewed against a completed close, estimated position risk including entry/exit costs, and remaining room under explicit assignment rules. Monitoring and a full exit are required for execution demonstration. Observation can be valid without an entry; it cannot substitute for execution evidence. Risk-sizing demonstration is required before entry practice.

These rubrics check explicit educational process rules. They do not establish suitability, a reliable live edge, optimal holding periods or future profitability. The option labs assess calculations and lifecycle decisions; the existing two-call sandbox remains a simplified order rehearsal rather than a complete options market simulator.

## Sources

Original examples and calculations were checked against SEC Investor.gov order-type, account, diversification and performance-claim resources; OCC/OIC Greeks, assignment and product-comparison resources. Source links are embedded in lessons. Regulator/broker account requirements should be rechecked; no fixed day-trading account threshold is encoded here.

The teaching progression uses worked examples, explanatory prompts, independent retrieval and delayed review, informed by the IES What Works Clearinghouse practice guide, “Organizing Instruction and Study to Improve Student Learning.” Educational research does not establish trading profitability.
